## Techniques

1. **Metonymy**: never film the whole action, only symbolic objects. One readable action per shot, in under a second.
2. **A pace of 12–17 frames per shot (25 fps)**, i.e. **15–20 frames at 30 fps**. A shot with nervous internal movement can run longer.
3. **Attack then stop**: a fast movement (slide, drop, object rushing in) followed by a near-frozen frame. Frame-to-frame difference drops from ≈9 to ≈0.4.
4. **Silence before the impact**: before each big sound, the level dips to −45 dB for 3–8 frames. That gap is what gives the impact its punch.
5. **Sound offset from the cut**: it lands 2–4 frames after the cut, or spills over the next shot, which ties the shots together.
6. **Climax before the exit**: the strongest impact comes right before the return to normal pace.
7. **Color and movement link the shots**: warm colors at departure, cold at arrival, with a hue match between shots. Movements chain in the same direction (vertical here).
8. **The slowdown is not slow motion**: no duplicated or interpolated frames. A static last shot, a light switching off and the sound dropping away bring the edit back to normal.

The duplicated frames every 24–25 frames are just the 24 → 25 fps conversion, not an effect.
